01 / THE IDEA

A brighter space
to learn and grow.

One learner, four complementary skills. A more connected approach to learning support.

LexiLand is a university research project developed at the Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology (SLIIT) under the supervision of Prof. Sanvitha Kasthuriarachchi and co-supervision of Ms. Thisara Shyamalee. It brings Working Memory, Phonological Awareness, Reading Processing and Speech Processing into a single animal-guided platform.

Each learning area connects identification activities with a suitable starting point, progressive practice and component-level progress reports. The platform is designed for learners, with parents and teachers supporting the journey.

LexiLand indicates possible support needs. It does not diagnose dyslexia.

BUNNY'S LEARNING AREA

Working Memory

Remember numbers, pictures and instructions, and practise putting information in order.

Explore component

Identification (screening)

A 15-question Memory Check covers pattern recognition, word memory, following instructions and number memory. Results highlight areas for practice.

Improvement (practice)

Four games per level: Picture Memory, Listening Game, Pattern Puzzle and Digit Span Memory. Each game contains five questions. Higher-level digit activities introduce backward recall.

Progression

Four colour-coded levels connect practice with feedback and recorded attempts. Exact allocation cut-offs and progression rules are awaiting final confirmation.

Analysis approach

Random Forest with recall, digit-span, sequence, instruction, pattern and response-time features. Balanced class weighting is recorded in the supplied model notes.

PARROT'S LEARNING AREA

Phonological Awareness

Explore sound–letter links, letter differences, spelling patterns and segmentation.

Explore component

Identification (screening)

A 15-question screening covers phonological awareness (5 questions), visual processing (6) and literacy skills (4). The materials describe multiple-choice and picture-selection tasks.

Improvement (practice)

Sound Detective, Picture Match, Count & Split and Word Play use sound–letter links, visual letter differences, counting and spelling tasks.

Progression

Four levels connect activities with feedback, stars and progress reports. Final level-map labels and allocation thresholds remain under confirmation.

Analysis approach

Random Forest with 16 score, time and efficiency features. A depth limit of five is recorded in the model notes.

Fernando K.H.D.M · IT22141088

ZEBRA'S LEARNING AREA

Reading Processing

Combine reading activities with webcam gaze information to guide support.

Explore component

Identification (screening)

Camera setup and gaze calibration lead into shapes and Sentence Safari tasks. Reading responses and gaze events provide complementary evidence.

Improvement (practice)

Word Match, Build the Word, Sentence Order and Story Question progress from words to short stories. Interfaces include listening, help and reading-ruler controls.

Progression

Four levels provide increasingly complex reading activities. Gaze information can prompt additional support; the final adaptation rules and model output labels require confirmation.

Analysis approach

MediaPipe-based gaze processing. The supplied materials describe both Random Forest and rule-based analysis; the final combination has not yet been confirmed.

Responsibility

Khan N.A.B · IT22266064

LION'S LEARNING AREA

Speech Processing

Record spoken responses and practise pronunciation and reading aloud with Leo.

Explore component

Identification (screening)

The learner views or listens to a prompt, records a spoken response and receives retry guidance if the recording is unsuitable. Speech analysis combines transcription and acoustic evidence.

Improvement (practice)

Leo’s Training Safari and Story Roar Trail guide recorded practice, sequential story activities and progress checkpoints against a baseline.

Progression

Practice follows a support-based path with checkpoints and rewards. The final activity mapping, prompt count and unlocking rules remain under confirmation.

Analysis approach

Whisper speech recognition and a Random Forest support model using 44 acoustic summary features. Optimised model results and their run evidence are awaiting verification.

Responsibility

Navod J.N · IT22027542

RESEARCH NOTEBOOK

Understanding
the learning journey.

Iterative design, development, evaluation and refinement, focused on an accessible learning experience for Sri Lankan primary students.

Design Science Research
Literature survey +

The project literature review considers working memory, phonological processing, reading behaviour and speech as complementary areas of evidence. It examines gamified screening, letter–sound learning and eye-tracking approaches, alongside the need for Sinhala-language support.

Representative studies include Swanson, Zheng & Jerman (2009) on memory and reading disabilities; Rello et al. (2020) on gamified screening; Nilsson Benfatto et al. (2016) on eye-tracking screening; and Richardson & Lyytinen (2014) on GraphoGame.

References are drawn from the supplied research content pack. The full bibliography is awaiting final verification.

Research gap +

The project investigates how to connect four learning areas, two languages and the stages of identification, practice and monitoring in one child-friendly platform. Its focus is a shared pathway for Grade 2–4 learners, rather than isolated activities that provide disconnected results.

Research problem +

How can an accessible Sinhala–English platform help identify which learning skills need support and connect that evidence with suitable, monitored practice for Sri Lankan primary learners?

The project addresses three linked needs: earlier identification of possible difficulties, component-specific information and continuity from screening to practice.

Research objectives +

Main objective: Develop a bilingual, game-based platform for screening possible dyslexia-related difficulties and providing personalised learning support to students aged 7–9 in Grades 2–4.

  1. Assess memory, sequencing and recall, and provide progressive activities.
  2. Assess sound, letter and spelling skills in Sinhala and English.
  3. Analyse reading accuracy and gaze for adaptive support.
  4. Identify pronunciation difficulties and provide personalised speech practice.
Methodology & evaluation +

The project follows Design Science Research: design, build, evaluate and refine. The documented learning workflow is Screen → Analyse → Personalise → Practise → Monitor.

Evaluation materials describe train/test splitting and five-fold cross-validation, alongside feedback used to simplify questionnaires and interactions. Final participant counts, dataset details and model results require team verification.

Results, limitations & next steps +

Model evaluation results are being finalised and will be published after verification. The supplied pack records conflicting runs and sample figures; no headline accuracy is presented here.

Key limitations include dataset size and generalisability, webcam lighting and calibration, microphone quality, background noise and speech-recognition coverage. Larger validation and extended language support are identified as future work.
